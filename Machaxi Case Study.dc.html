Chapter One

The vision, and the product standing in its way

Machaxi was building Vision AI: a system that watched sessions and analysed technique move by move. Real coaching intelligence — the clearest differentiator the product could have.

But intelligence isn't the same as coaching. The AI could see and score; it couldn't, on its own, decide what to say to a learner or how to say it. Dropped into the interface as raw output, it would read as a machine handing down judgment on someone's backhand. Held back entirely, it would waste the product's most valuable asset. The design problem was neither — it was the shape in between.

A container built for a different job

Machaxi Learn was a plan-management tool: attendance, schedules, leave compensation, membership pausing. Competent admin. No entry point for a new learner. No context in which any AI feedback would land. The scale of the operational brief made it heavier still — the PRD covered player selection, skill level, time slots, centres, sessions per week, fixed versus custom schedules, discounts, and validity windows. And three users sat on top of it at any time: the learner, the parent booking on their behalf, and the coach on the other side of the session. Every screen had to work across all three.

My role

I set the human-AI framing that determined what Vision AI would and wouldn't say to a learner — and translated the operational PRD and the AI system's outputs into product structure, flow logic, wireframes, and final UI direction. I worked closely with the Machaxi PM and the AI team throughout, pushing signal decisions back into the AI scope where a learner-useful output was missing.

Chapter Two

Five blockers

Auditing the legacy experience, every gap was the same gap in different clothes: each one cut the legs out from under Vision AI before it could say anything.

01

No entry point

Nothing introduced Learn as a structured coaching product. A first-time visitor got a tool that assumed enrollment. You can't coach someone who hasn't been convinced to show up.

02

Zero decision support

Choosing a sport, level, schedule, tier — the purchase entry was a form, not a flow. And every question it failed to ask was context the AI would never have.

03

Generic plan logic

A badminton plan and a swimming plan don't work the same way. Flattening them into one shape meant coaching feedback could only ever be one-size-fits-nobody.

04

Utility-first framing

Post-enrollment, the product led with admin. Vision AI was about to generate progress; the interface was built to display paperwork. Dropped into that frame, the smartest insight in the world reads as a bolt-on.

05

Homeless AI outputs

Technique analysis, error detection, drill recommendations — being generated, going nowhere. Presented raw, they'd expose the model. Presented as coaching, they were the whole point.

Chapter Three

The reframe

One decision organised everything else: reframe the product from plan management into a full coaching lifecycle — and treat Vision AI as one layer inside it, not the layer. Three shifts carried it:

From

Plan management

To

Coaching lifecycle — discovery, sport-specific enrollment, sessions, progress, carrying a learner from first curiosity into an ongoing relationship

From

Generic membership

To

Sport-specific journeys — each sport gets the purchase journey its reality demands, which also hands Vision AI the context that keeps feedback from going generic

From

Admin layer

To

Coaching surface — the utilities stay, the hierarchy flips, so AI signals land as coaching rather than noise

Directions considered, and rejected

01
A single universal purchase flow
Badminton is a recurring weekly plan; swimming is sessions with a validity window. One flow would have forced either sport into a shape it doesn't fit — and stripped the AI of the sport-specific context its feedback relied on.
02
Vision AI as a standalone insights dashboard
Adding an "AI Analytics" screen to the existing product would have solved for feature-checkbox visibility and nothing else. The AI would have read as a bolt-on, and the plan-management framing would have kept every problem below it intact.
03
Keep it to a purchase-flow redesign
There was real scope pressure to fix the front door and leave the post-enrollment experience alone — a smaller, safer, faster project. But Vision AI wouldn't survive it: its outputs would still be arriving into an admin console. The coaching-surface reframe had to come with it, or the AI layer couldn't work.

Chapter Four

The foundation

Five decisions built the ground floor for a coaching product.

Decision 01

Start with the learner

Login, player creation, sport, goals, location, proficiency — captured before anything is sold. Households with multiple players get clean navigation, and Vision AI gets the one thing it can't work without: a person to be specific about.

Onboarding: player creation, sport selection, and proficiency capture

Decision 02

Sport-specific purchase architecture

Two genuinely different journeys, not one flow with two skins. Everything downstream — scheduling, pricing, progress, AI feedback — inherits a structure that matches the sport's reality.

Purchase journeys shaped by how each sport is actually bought

Decision 03

Conviction before commitment

Sport landing pages that make the case: coaching benefits, expected outcomes, workshop previews, plan structures, trial entry points. The product argues for itself before it asks for money.

Sport landing page: benefits, testimonials, AI analytics preview, trial entry

Decision 04

Nothing hidden at checkout

Player, sport, level, centre, schedule, discounts, exact start date, final cost — all of it explicit. A product that's transparent about what it charges has standing to be believed about what it's coaching.

Checkout: every decision surfaced explicitly

Decision 05

Flip the post-enrollment home

The new home leads with upcoming sessions, attendance streaks, curriculum milestones, and coach feedback. Compensation, leave, rewards — still there, one layer down. An admin console became a coaching environment.

Post-enrollment home: sessions, streaks, curriculum milestones, coach feedback

None of the operational utilities were removed. Membership pausing, compensation booking, rewards, curriculum tracking, coach feedback — all preserved, all repositioned. What changed was the hierarchy: they're still in the product, just no longer the first thing a new learner encounters.

Chapter Five

The AI coaching layer

Vision AI's job was to see and score. The design's job was to decide what a learner should hear from it, and in what voice.

One input among three

The decision that shaped everything below: Vision AI is one input among three. Its signal is presented alongside two other inputs the learner already trusts — the coach's experience (session notes, direct feedback) and the athlete's own effort (attendance, streaks, curriculum progress). No single input carries the verdict. The AI reinforces; it doesn't rule.

The session summary is where a learner meets Vision AI first. Rather than a single AI-generated verdict, the summary reads across three parallel signals: the coach's note from the session, the athlete's own effort record, and Vision AI's technique observations. Together they cohere. Alone, none of them speaks with more authority than it's earned.

Vision AI insight layer: performance scores, technique breakdowns, error detection with paired drills

Six moves built on that framing

01

Raw model data stays out

No confidence scores, no per-move detection logs, no "the AI flagged this 7 times." Those are internal signals, kept internal by design. What surfaces is coaching — patterns, trends, category-level observations.

02

Technique broken down by category

Feedback aggregates upward — from move-level analysis into categories like grip, footwork, positioning, follow-through. Each category shows the learner's strength as a shape rather than a raw percentage, so the read is "footwork is holding you back this month" rather than "footwork score: 47." Coaching is a pattern; the interface reflects that.

03

Errors arrive with their fix

When Vision AI identifies something worth flagging, it's paired with a drill. The card leads with the diagnosis in plain language — "cross-court timing is early on your backhand" — and drops the recommended drill directly beneath, with a "Watch the moment" link to the video evidence. An error framed as a next step is coaching; framed as a verdict, it's a judgment.

04

Video-linked evidence

Where feedback references a specific moment, the learner can watch it. The player is scrubbed to the frame Vision AI referenced, with a subtle overlay marking what was detected. The AI's read is checkable — which is how a coaching signal earns weight without asking to be believed on the model's authority alone.

05

Feedback in a coach's voice

Post-session summaries are written to feel like a message from a coach, not a system readout. The opening line names one thing the learner did well; the second names one thing to work on; the third points to tomorrow's drill. The signal is machine-made; the voice, framing, and sequence are design decisions.

06

Progress over time

Improvement arcs across sessions show where a learner is climbing and where a weakness keeps showing up. The chart's dominant read is the trend line, not any single session's score. Trend-level information — where the AI is at its most useful — sits in front of any single moment.

Chapter Six

Delivered vs. open

Reported outcome

8–10%

lift in overall retention post-launch, per the client's internal reporting

Delivered

End-to-end redesign — discovery, sport-specific purchase flows, staged decision architecture, the rebuilt post-enrollment home, and the Vision AI insight layer designed around the human-AI framing.

Still open

Deeper diagnostics on which parts of the redesign are moving the retention number — the purchase reframe, the coaching-lifecycle home, or the AI layer specifically. And the between-session touchpoints that would compound the effect further.

Chapter Seven

Reflections

01The product lives between sessions. Retention for a coaching product is mostly decided outside the facility. Nudges, check-ins, drill reminders were scoped as phase two; they belong beside the core flows, because between sessions is where a coaching relationship either compounds or evaporates.
02Design for how AI actually shows up, not how it's pitched. The hardest work on this project wasn't the analytics screen — it was deciding what Vision AI shouldn't say, and building a product credible enough that the parts it did say landed as coaching. Next time I'd start there on day one, not arrive at it midway.
03The AI waited on the product. Building the coaching foundation before Vision AI could ship was the right call for the learner — but it left the AI team's work sitting in a queue for months. A version of this project that parallel-tracked one live AI surface early would have surfaced integration issues sooner and given the AI team a real target to build against. Right sequencing for the user; a real cost to the team, and worth naming.

Core principle

Product maturity isn't always about adding functionality — it's often about reframing what the product is asking the user to do. Machaxi Learn already had the features. What it needed was a coherent story about what those features added up to, told in the right order, to the right people, at the right moment. That's a product problem, not a UI problem.
